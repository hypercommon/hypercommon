% Lattice-like real networks (Sec. sec:reallattice of the main text)
\label{app:reallattice}

Every kind follows one recipe. Nodes lie along a chain, on a sheet or on a map, each
node is linked to a few nearby nodes that it resembles most, and the recorded groups are
stretches of the chain or patches of the sheet, so the groups are expected to be
lattice-like. All rules (selection of graphs, edges, groups and controls) were fixed and
written down before any detection was run. Each graph has a \emph{control} with the same
nodes and groups whose edges come from position alone, which removes the data signal and
keeps the geometry. The build scripts (\texttt{paper/analysis/*\_build.py}), the sources
with their checksums and licences, and the graphs are in the repository. Groups with
fewer than three nodes are dropped; detection, grids and scoring are those of the
smaller real networks (Sec.~\ref{sec:real}), with HyperCommon's threshold searched over
100 quantile levels of its triple scores.

\emph{Hi-C contact maps}~\cite{rao2014hic}: GM12878 chromosomes 17--22 at 25\,kb,
nodes the covered bins, each bin linked to the $k\in\{8,16,32\}$ bins with which it has
the most normalised contacts; groups the Arrowhead contact domains, which are nested.
Control: the $k$ nearest bins along the chromosome.
\emph{Pen sketches}~\cite{ha2018sketch}: 20 Quick, Draw!\ drawings of five categories,
points resampled along the strokes, each linked to its $k\in\{8,16\}$ nearest points in
position and pen time; groups the strokes. Control: position only.
\emph{Images}~\cite{martin2001database,arbelaez2011contour}: 15 BSDS500 test images in
blocks of $6\times6$ pixels, each block linked to the $k$ blocks of most similar colour
within three blocks; groups the segments of all human annotators, pooled. Control: the
$k$ nearest blocks on the grid.
\emph{Pedestrian tracks}~\cite{milan2016mot16}: 15 frame windows of the MOT17 training
sequences, nodes the annotated pedestrian boxes, each linked to the $k=8$ boxes nearby
in space and time that overlap it most; groups the tracks. Control: the nearest boxes.
\emph{Commuting maps}~\cite{census_commuting,ons_odwp01ew}: US counties, and local
authorities and small areas (MSOAs) of England and Wales, each unit linked to the $k$
units it exchanges most commuters with, after a coverage normalisation; groups states
or commuting zones (US), regions (local authorities) and local authority districts
(MSOAs). The commuting zones are themselves built from commuting flows, so that set is
partly circular. Control: the $k$ nearest units on the map.
\emph{Protein contacts}~\cite{sillitoe2021cath}: 60 chains with two or more CATH
domains, residues linked when their C$_\alpha$ atoms are within $8$\,\AA\ (and
$7$\,\AA); groups the domains. Control: residues within $h$ positions along the chain,
$h$ matching the mean degree.
\emph{Time series}~\cite{ermshaus2023clasp}: 24 series of the Time Series Segmentation
Benchmark with two to nine segments, nodes sliding windows, each linked to the $k$
windows of most similar shape (among all windows, or among those nearby in time);
groups the annotated segments. Control: the nearest windows in time.
\emph{Brain tissue}~\cite{maynard2021transcriptome}: the 12 Visium slices of human
dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, spots linked to the $k$ of their 36 nearest spots with
the most similar expression (30 principal components); groups the manually annotated
layers, or their spatially connected parts. Control: the nearest spots on the grid.
\emph{Articles}: 15 English Wikipedia featured articles, sentences in reading order, each
linked to the $k$ most similar (TF--IDF cosine) of the 40 sentences before and after it;
groups the sections and subsections, nested. Control: the nearest sentences in reading
order.
\emph{Transit networks}: the rail (or rail and central bus) networks of 12 cities from
their official GTFS feeds, stations linked when they are within two (or three) stops of
each other on a route; groups the routes, which share their interchange stations.
\emph{Street maps}~\cite{haklay2008openstreetmap}: 12 city centres from OpenStreetMap,
points every $30$\,m or less along named streets, linked when within two (or three)
steps along the street network; groups the named streets, which share their crossings.
For both, the control links each node to its nearest nodes by distance.

On Hi-C maps, sketches, images, proteins, time series, articles, transit networks and
brain tissue at $k=8$, HyperCommon is far more accurate on the data graphs than on their
controls. On pedestrian tracks, street maps and three of the five commuting maps the
control is about as accurate, so there the groups are found from position alone
(Table~\ref{tab:S-real-lattice-sets}).
